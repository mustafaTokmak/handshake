**My lens first — the parts count.** The demo-critical live chain is: world+board, actor, ledger, classifier, inverse synthesiser, planner/conflict, executor, plan-table UI, `/reset`, judge-edit path = **10 components, 3 of them LLM calls that must each produce a *specific* output** (exactly one IRREVERSIBLE, exactly one CONFLICT). The narrow polished demos I described in round 1 have a 3–4 deep chain with 0–1 LLM calls. So yes: **the panel quoted the ambition warning and then spec'd an ambitious demo.** It's partially saved — the never-cut list is the actual product and the cut order is correctly sequenced — but as written, this is not the narrow demo. The subset is in #6.

**1. The strongest case against.** The wow is a *delta between two states, on a world judges know is synthetic, for a pain they must imagine having*. Three compounding taxes: **memory tax** (must recall the before-state to feel the undo), **trust tax** (you control both sides of the problem — the ledger only exists because you built the world to be ledger-able; the first Q&A knife is "Salesforce won't give you before/after snapshots or typed inverses, what then?"), and **attention tax** (a judge who tunes in at 0:50 missed the red phase, so the green phase is meaningless). SIEGE pays none of these — tiles are stateless, tune-in-anytime. The panel correctly chose controllable engineering risk over uncontrollable experimental risk, but underpriced *legibility* risk, which is the risk that actually kills demo #12.

**2. Most likely failure: 15:30, first full integration rehearsal.** The actor is a free LLM agent, so its 14 calls vary run-to-run — different fields, different counts, different email wording. Therefore the plan table varies: 13 rows instead of 11, zero conflicts, the email classified *compensable* instead of irreversible. The demo script written against the 13:00 fixture no longer matches live behavior, and the team burns 16:30–18:00 — the credibility-beats slot — pinning the mess down, arriving at "script the actor" 90 minutes late and under-rehearsed. What precisely breaks: the seam between a non-deterministic actor and a verbatim-rehearsed demo. It's the highest-damage failure because it's the only major one with **no line in the cut order**. Runner-up: 20:0x, the judge-edit beat — an untrained participant edits the wrong field or one the planner doesn't version-check. Default to a team member doing the edit; take a judge only if one volunteers early.

**3. Legibility at demo #12: lands, conditionally.** The plan table with before→after columns *is* the counterfactual externalized — the audience never has to hold the before in their head if every row shows it. That's the hidden weapon: the wow isn't "watch undo," it's "here is a typed receipt of everything the agent did, with an undo button per line." Receipts are legible to enterprise judges; it's literally Conduct's pitch. Two things in the plan actively hurt it: the **35 seconds of presenter silence** (works in film, fatal at demo #12 — narrate stakes instead: "that's a refund. that's a deleted customer. that's an email nobody can unsend"), and the board must render **objects** ("Acme Ltd — active, £12k ARR") not events, so "before" is a picture, not a memory. Point at the email row first (legibility anchor for everyone), the conflict row second (credibility anchor for engineers). And cap it at two wows — the "undo is undoable" Logfire punchline is a 10-second throwaway, not a third act.

**4. Was SIEGE wrongly rejected? No — but the panel located its risk wrong.** The breach rate is *not* outside team control: you control both swarm and target, so you tune defenses into the 5–15% band, and you can calibrate before Saturday. SIEGE's real kill shots are different: (a) the wow is a **self-graded number** — "your scanner says your agent is safer per your scanner" dies in Q&A exactly as hard as Unwind's circularity; (b) its most valuable artifact, the auto-patch, is a **diff** — the least stage-legible artifact in existence; (c) it's the default 2026 shape — with Modal credits in the room, expect 2–3 other agent-vs-agent projects. If pre-building were clearly allowed and the target were already calibrated, SIEGE would be the better stage demo. Under pre-build ambiguity and the 09:35 stranger-team constraint, Unwind stands. Under-stated point in Unwind's favor: it's **robust to the team lottery** — three lanes separated by a frozen JSON contract parallelize cleanly across strangers; SIEGE's calibration loop needs tight coupling.

**5. Highest-leverage change: script the actor.** A fixed, versioned scenario file — 14 calls, exact args — with Gemini Flash generating only the *reasoning narration* around each call. This makes the plan table deterministic, so the demo script is writable at 15:00 and rehearsable verbatim; makes the 13:00 fixture byte-identical to the live run, so the mid-sentence fallback is actually seamless; protects the 16:30 credibility slot; and costs nothing on stage — no judge can tell, and the Q&A answer is strong *and true*: "Unwind operates on the ledger; it doesn't care who wrote the calls." One decision kills the #1 failure mode and shortens the live chain from 10 components to ~6.

**6. Verdict: BUILD UNWIND — the subset.** Four tools (CRM, Billing, Email, Files; cut Calendar and Messaging), scripted actor, ledger, classifier→synthesiser→planner, **2-pane UI** (world board + plan table; the Logfire tab *is* the trace pane), irreversible-email beat, conflict beat with team-member default, `/reset`, recorded fallback. Modal hosts the world (sponsor surface preserved) but fork-and-replay leaves the live path — run the dry-run against a local snapshot copy, same code, no live Modal dependency; demo it only if it's solid by 17:00. Cut the GitHub revert entirely unless you're ahead at 17:15 — the email row already carries the irreversibility emotion; a revert commit is a second illustration of the same point. Every sponsor integration survives (DeepMind models, Modal hosting, Pydantic AI + Logfire, Conduct pitch), and the live chain drops to 6 deep with 2 LLM calls. *That* is the narrow polished demo. The plan as written is the ambitious one wearing its clothes.
