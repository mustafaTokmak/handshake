Jev-first hackathon ideas · 19 September 2026

What becomes worth building when judgment is fast and cheap?

Five ideas where frequent evaluation is the product. All can use Jev as the only AI model in the core demo: ordinary code supplies state, choices, arithmetic and actions; Jev supplies semantic judgments.

The baseline we actually have

Your JEV-API-REFERENCE.md records 374–483 ms and $0.000021 per call containing four judgments, from small smoke runs. That extrapolates to $0.021 per 1,000 similar calls or $21 per million. It is a workload estimate, not a fixed price or throughput guarantee.

The official docs confirm that independent questions share one state and execute in parallel, with little added latency. The launch post advertises $0.042 per million input tokens and free output tokens. Longer states and additional question text can change billing; do not assume unlimited questions are free. Build for roughly half-second judgments until measured otherwise.

01 · My pick for a memorable demo

Live Crowd — an audience that reacts while you edit

Product: A launch-message editor with 24 fictional audience cards. As you revise the offer, the crowd changes: understands it, finds it relevant, sees an objection, or needs more information. You see which specific audience groups a sentence loses.

Why speed and cost matter: This needs dozens of judgments after every meaningful edit. Slow responses make it a report; cheap, concurrent responses make it an interactive instrument. Each persona is a separate compact state, with four questions bundled per call.

Three-minute demo: Start with a confusing product offer. Edit one sentence. Watch audience tiles update as responses arrive. Change the price and reveal which objections shift. Click a tile to see the exact persona attributes and named judgment probabilities, without inventing a generated explanation.

Small build: One editor, 24 curated personas, concurrent calls with a limit, 600 ms debounce and stale-response rejection. At 24 calls × 30 edits, the small-call baseline is about $0.015 per session. Actual persona payloads may cost more; a whole crowd may take longer than one call.

What to prove: Is it sensitive to useful edits rather than just positive wording? These are simulated reactions, not real customer research or conversion forecasts. Show contradictory cases, and label the audience synthetic.

02 · Strongest enterprise use

Meaning Triggers — automation that listens for intent

Product: Let an operator define triggers such as “someone is blocked waiting for another team” or “this customer describes the same unresolved issue again.” Every incoming event is evaluated against the trigger set. Code routes matches into existing workflows.

Why speed and cost matter: Evaluation belongs on every event, before the next workflow step. One bundle checks multiple semantic conditions without a serial model call per rule. Cheap evaluation makes quiet, high-volume streams worth monitoring.

Three-minute demo: Replay a support stream. A request that contains none of the configured keywords still activates the right trigger. Add another semantic rule and measure the change in latency and cost rather than claiming it is zero.

Small build: A webhook simulator, six rules, an event stream, and reversible queue routing. Use explicit local context for “same unresolved issue”; Jev does not retrieve history itself. At 100,000 small events, the baseline is $2.10, before larger rule bundles and infrastructure.

What to prove: Measure false positives on unseen paraphrases and near-misses. Use ordinary code for dates, identity and counts. The demo routes items; it does not autonomously send customer messages.

03 · Best compact build

Adaptive Intake — the form that asks only what is missing

Product: A bug-report intake that reacts as someone describes a problem. Jev checks whether the description supplies reproduction steps, expected behavior, actual behavior and environment. Completed questions disappear; the next relevant question appears.

Why speed and cost matter: The judgment runs repeatedly while typing, so users can see the form get shorter as they answer. Cheap calls remove the incentive to wait until submission or batch everything into a slow conversational turn.

Three-minute demo: Paste “it crashes.” Four missing-information cards appear. Expand it into a useful report and watch the cards clear. Add an ambiguous statement and keep that card open. No chatbot and no generated text are needed.

Small build: A fixed bank of eight questions, four Jev checks per call, 500–800 ms debounce, and deterministic next-question selection. Forty calls per completed intake cost about $0.00084 at the baseline; 10,000 intakes cost about $8.40.

What to prove: Detect whether information is meaningfully present, not just whether a keyword appears. Keep manual navigation available when the model misses it. This is the simplest idea to complete well in one day, though less visually unusual than Live Crowd.

04 · Best demonstration of cheap scale

Living Feed — a filter defined by meaning

Product: A feed whose filter is “show developers who tried a workaround and are still blocked,” rather than a keyword search. Each new forum post or support message receives several reusable semantic attributes; the interface updates continuously.

Why speed and cost matter: Low per-item cost makes evaluation of every incoming item plausible. Parallel requests make backfilling a selected corpus practical, subject to quotas. After the attributes are stored, sliders and combinations run locally and instantly.

Three-minute demo: Stream 200 prepared posts into a board. Watch relevant posts surface despite different wording. Adjust the weighting of “has tried a fix” versus “still blocked” and reorder instantly without another model call. Introduce a genuinely new criterion and show the re-evaluation progress honestly.

Small build: Three fixed semantic dimensions, one local dataset, a worker queue and a live board. At 10,000 short posts the baseline is $0.21. A new rubric requires new evaluation; one giant corpus prompt is not the architecture.

What to prove: Compare precision against keyword matching on labeled examples. Keep source text visible. The differentiator is continuous, editable semantic filtering, not a claim that semantic search itself is new.

05 · Best fit with the agentic hackathon

Agent Flight Recorder — measure every step, not a sample

Product: A live timeline that marks whether each agent step advances the stated objective, repeats a failed approach, introduces an unsupported assertion, or leaves an explicit constraint unresolved. A replay reveals where an otherwise successful-looking run began drifting.

Why speed and cost matter: Evaluating every step becomes affordable, and results arrive while the run is still active. Bundle checks per compact transition. The observer can run asynchronously so its latency does not block the agent.

Three-minute demo: Replay two agent traces that end with the same “done” message. One made progress; the other repeated failed actions. Their timelines visibly diverge. Clicking a marker shows the relevant instruction and tool result.

Small build: Two stored traces, four checks, a streaming replay, and a deterministic pause suggestion after repeated concerning readings. At 100,000 small transitions, the baseline is $2.10; real tool results may be much longer.

What to prove: Compare flags against human-labeled traces. Treat this as noisy observability, not proof of agent correctness. Keep exact tool failures and repeated IDs as code-derived facts; ask Jev only about semantic relationships.

Pick Live Crowd for the stage. Its effect is visible immediately, and frequent parallel judgments are central to the experience. Choose Adaptive Intake if build time is tight, or Agent Flight Recorder if the priority is demonstrating a concrete agent-development tool.

Before committing: measure one representative payload at concurrency 1, 8 and 24; record latency distribution, actual billed cost, errors and judgment quality. The docs establish feasibility, but the existing smoke test does not establish sustained throughput or quality for these domains.
