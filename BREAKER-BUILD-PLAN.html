The demo-worthy decision

Scenario: a support agent interprets a customer's refund request. Code already has an authenticated customer, a specific payment and verified amount; Jev assesses whether the proposed action matches the request. This avoids asking a model to discover identities or perform invoice arithmetic. Gemini plans and can request clarification when halted.

For one explicit failure event—“this refund is outside the customer's request”—define estimated exposure = P(outside_request) × refund_amount. The loss assumption is that an incorrect refund loses its full amount, with no recovery. This is a model-based estimate, not validated expected loss on the business distribution. Neither account value nor hypothetical email damage is a defensible substitute for that loss.

Use exhaustive classes within_request, outside_request and insufficient_evidence. For the first prototype, any material insufficient-evidence mass triggers a halt, using a threshold chosen before the held-out evaluation. The displayed outside-request estimate excludes unknown outcomes: say so. Do not multiply independent marginal answers together to invent a joint probability.

Smallest credible implementation

  1. One workflow, two tools: propose a refund; create a clarification draft. The draft stays local. A simulated payment ledger records committed refunds and a local outbox records drafts. All money effects are explicitly simulated.
  2. Typed proposal: proposal ID, customer ID, payment ID, amount in integer pence, currency, expected record version, request text and evidence references. Code checks permissions, ID relationships, remaining refundable balance, duplicate action keys and amounts before semantic evaluation.
  3. Immutable evaluation: Jev receives a small snapshot. Persist its raw result, model ID, rubric hash, state hash, elapsed time and decision reason. Validate finite probabilities in [0,1] and a normalized distribution. Missing fields, malformed responses and timeouts halt the action.
  4. Central commit: one ledger owner rechecks version, balance, policy version and action key immediately before writing. A changed snapshot requires reevaluation. Evaluation workers never own payment credentials or mutate the ledger.
  5. Bounded rewrite: Gemini receives the halt reason and can propose a corrected action or clarification draft once. Any new proposal goes through the same checks. The model cannot approve its own bypass.
  6. Small display: two large readings—autonomous actions / total proposals and estimated exposure accepted. Highlight the blocked action, its amount and plain-language reason. Show simulated/live/recorded mode persistently. Keep raw evidence expandable.

Use both a per-action ceiling and an optional run budget. Reserve exposure atomically when accepting a proposal and record it once on commit; duplicates consume neither money nor budget twice. Changing the policy creates a new version and reevaluates pending proposals. A slider previews decisions on stored evaluations and does not execute anything.

Hour one: a decision gate, not a calibration claim

  • Make one authenticated Jev call through the intended route and inspect raw output without logging secrets. Verify that class probabilities survive the route. Load the official TypeSafe integration skill before writing its adapter.
  • Prepare a development set and separately authored held-out cases: clear requests, clear mismatches, missing context, misleading text, near-duplicate names, and explicit structured-ID contradictions. Human labels must not come from the same model being tested.
  • Choose rubric and policy thresholds on the development set. Measure held-out unsafe approvals, safe-action coverage, abstentions, and p50/p95 latency. Repeated calls measure repeatability, not additional independent examples.
  • For demo readiness require zero unsafe approvals on the small held-out set and useful autonomous coverage (initial target: at least half of clearly safe cases). This is a smoke-test criterion, not evidence of production safety or calibration. Report the denominator.
  • If access or output shape fails, retain the app with a labeled fixture replay. If judgments fail, use deterministic policy and explicit uncertainty stops; remove claims of calibrated autonomy. Do not spend the afternoon pivoting into robotics or rebuilding Unwind.
